Every DraftKings market for this week's games, ranked by how likely the bet is to win. Each card shows three things: the win chance, how solid the evidence is, and whether DraftKings' price is attractive, fair or poor. The bot's picks come first: the bets a careful analyst would take, by category, with a button that combines them into the top six.

How the bets are ranked

Win chance. Starts from the whole market: every book's price with its margin removed, moved onto DraftKings' line, with Pinnacle counting double. The simulation (100,000 drive-by-drive games per matchup, built from matchups, pace, quarterbacks, usage, injuries, rest and weather) then moves that chance by up to 4 points on player props and half totals, less when the two disagree by more than 10 points and not at all past 20. Game lines (moneyline, spread, total) take the market's chance unchanged, because in the walk-forward test the simulation did not beat the closing line.

Evidence confidence rates the quality of the inputs, not the chance of winning: it counts the books pricing the market, how closely the simulation and the market agree, how much current-season data stands behind both teams, and flags (a quarterback change, a questionable player, a one-sided price, a player with no independent projection).

Bot's picks runs an analyst's checks on every bet and keeps the best per category: this season's form against the line, the opponent's rank for that stat, the game script the spread implies, receivers or blockers out, workload shifts from teammates out, wind, the quarterback situation, the price's age, and how far the simulation sits from the market. A failed hard check (form pointing the other way, a questionable player, a non-starter, a 15-point model/market gap) removes the bet; soft checks move its score. "Combine into the top 6" takes the six highest-scoring legs across the lists, one per game, at real DraftKings prices between 1.35 and 2.20; anytime touchdowns are never in the lists (they have their own board).

Price. Attractive: DraftKings pays at least 2% more than the win chance justifies (5% for anytime touchdowns, whose margin has to be assumed). Fair: within 3% of fair. Poor: worse than that. Unreliable: too few books, a long shot past +700, or books more than 25 points apart. Kicker field goals and alternate lines come from the simulation only (DraftKings' prices for them are not in the feed), so those cards show fair odds to compare by hand. Attractively priced bets lock at kickoff and are graded in the Results tab.

What was measured before the numbers were produced: each team's drive results against what the league scores from the same situations, pace, passing, kicking, the quarterback, the injury report and the context of the game. Sample sizes are shown; every multiplier is shrunk toward the league in proportion to how little data stands behind it.

Game dossiers

Every selection the model prices, in every market, nothing hidden. The "win" column is the simulation's own number; the EV column judges the price on the market's fair chance (all books, no vig) wherever DraftKings lists the bet. The likeliest bet is not the best bet: start from the Best bets tab.

How the grades work

Grades are expected value at the book's price, judged on the market's fair chance. The bar is +2% (+5% for anytime touchdowns, whose one-sided prices need an assumed margin): S clears it by 3 points or more, A clears it, B positive but under the bar, or a long shot or thin market that is never listed, C 0 to -4%, D worse than -4%. A selection with no market consensus (the book does not list it, or too few books do) is judged on the model alone and never graded above C: in the walk-forward test the model did not beat the closing lines, and in 2026 it under-projected high-volume quarterbacks by about 20 yards. No price means value unknown (C).

The "backtest" column shows how often selections the model rated at about the same probability actually won over 2023 to 2025, against each player's recent average (not a sportsbook line).
